# Player-built bearing mechanisms

The 8–9 November user correction replaces further bespoke UFO turret development with Create-style construction from separate blocks. Use that assembly principle inside the existing BlockBody/D6 system; no Create dependency or second physics engine was added. The old UFO kit remains compatible and its earlier evidence remains historical regression evidence, not generic-mechanism acceptance.

Two real components (`mechanical_bearing`, `rotary_drive`) and a craftable `mechanism_wrench` now work on ordinary physical bodies. Russian/English names, tooltips, recipes, drops, blockstates, block and item models are supplied. The initial models reuse vanilla iron/piston/copper/observer textures; custom art remains open. There is no generated turret blueprint in this workflow.

World construction: right-click two corners with the wrench to convert the actual selected world material into one existing BlockBody. Each axis is bounded to 16 cells (4096 examined cells maximum), and the entire selection validates loaded geometry, permissions, supported states and block-entity exclusion before mutation. Sneak-click cancels. This is explicit cuboid selection, not yet Create-style glue/connectivity extraction or disassembly controls.

On physical bodies, two wrench clicks connect meeting, opposing bearing faces on distinct ordinary bodies. Material points must already coincide within 4cm and normals within the existing orientation tolerance; connecting assigns no pose or velocity. Occupied, stale, unloaded, changed, unreachable or unauthorized anchors are rejected. The client uses the existing revisioned local-block packets and server ray validation. Sneak with a BlockItem retains placement, including local-face bearing/drive orientation, while the wrench retains its own sneak controls.

A separate correctly oriented drive behind either bearing supplies at most 40 torque units and speed 0/.2/.4/.6rad/s. Clicking cycles speed; sneak-click reverses. Off or a destroyed/misaligned drive releases power and preserves the passive bearing and actual momenta. Competing sources do not multiply torque. Sneak-clicking a bearing disconnects it without resetting motion. Destroyed/replaced bearing material removes the actual attachment.

The persistent existing joint registry owns the link and bounded motor command. An optional `blockDriven` marker defaults false in old saves and survives configuration and fracture rebinding. Generic blocks refresh their own links once per existing ready-island solve, before forces integrate; old UFO/operator links retain their existing controller. Connected bodies keep their ordinary physical collisions; generic hinges do not inherit the reference UFO kit's pair-wide collision exclusion. No per-block rigid bodies, follow teleports or visual-only rotors are introduced. Players can compose multiple axes from separate existing bodies.
