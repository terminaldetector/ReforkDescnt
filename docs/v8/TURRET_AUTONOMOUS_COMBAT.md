## Physical control and authority

The shared server target remains a naturally acquired external Survival/Adventure player. The real child must still be the carrier's installed module, with its actual surviving weapons and valid physical connection. Commands use the actual hinge frame and signed shortest angular error. Material bearing-rate feed-forward accounts for target motion, muzzle-point velocity and the carrier's rotating frame. Motor limits remain 0.6rad/s and 40Nm; existing engine, contact and solver budgets are unchanged. Overhead/degenerate bearing does not invent a second joint axis.

Carrier swing aligns the turret's aiming plane rather than incorrectly aligning the carrier's nose after the child has already turned. The physical yaw motor retains the azimuth degree of freedom. Real completed bursts and a bounded 160-tick fallback share aiming time with the rigid lasers/rockets. No target pose or body velocity is assigned by the AI. Actual emission still uses the current child's barrel quaternion, inherits `v + omega cross r`, applies point recoil and reconciles the existing passive constraints once per actual bank. It checks loaded terrain, source/other hulls and living bystanders. The physical child owns the projectile UUID/damage; no proxy LivingEntity or fabricated pilot is created.

Ordinary ServerPlayer movement arrives through position packets, and an impulse can remain in its velocity field after observed movement stops. A per-world server-frame observation now supplies continuous ordinary motion without editing the player. Identity, world/respawn continuity and large transfers are checked; repeated observation in one frame is idempotent. Unknown continuity falls back to the existing velocity model. Boarding state, free 6DoF and local-gravity motion retain their authoritative physical XYZ velocity. The bounded history drops removed players and clears on world/server unload.

Capture/explicit disable retires the autonomous motor command without resetting either body's momentum. A valid pilot's manual motor input cannot be overwritten by the AI. Game-mode changes are checked again at the articulated command/fire boundary. Only the actual installed controller object can mutate its joint or detach its body; an NBT reference copy remains read-only with respect to that assembly.

A real reactor cut can move the bearing's parent anchor onto a newly detached physical roof fragment. The old controller's root-to-child lookup then correctly fails. Previously that left the registry's old motor speed/force active. Retirement now finds its former bearing identity, removes its power and preserves the real joint, child state and fragment. It does not silently reconnect or recreate a missing reactor/module.

## Failures and proof scope

The original unpiloted module emitted 0 lasers/0 rockets. All first implementation and diagnostic failures remain archived. The initial carrier/child aim decomposition was wrong, battery scheduling could starve the module, and a proportional motor alone lagged a moving bearing. Actual lane diagnostics then found stale ordinary-player impulse velocity predicting displacement while the player's coordinates did not move. Observation-based ordinary motion allowed the real elevated-side test to fire both banks without changing its measured window.
