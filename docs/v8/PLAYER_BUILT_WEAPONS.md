# Player-built mounted weapons — 9 November 2026

This extends ordinary player-built mechanisms on the existing BlockBody/D6 solver, following the user's Create-style assembly direction. It adds no Create dependency, separate physics engine or new UFO turret blueprint. The previous bearing/drive/wrench increment is documented in [PLAYER_BUILT_MECHANISMS.md](PLAYER_BUILT_MECHANISMS.md).

## Implementation

The existing `ship_laser` and `ship_rocket` IDs now have six real local barrel directions, matching blockstate/model rotations and local-face placement on moving bodies. Missing direction properties in old saved palettes retain SOUTH, the previous local +Z axis. Old IDs, models and reference batteries remain compatible.

An ordinary vanilla lever mounted on the actual gun's support face switches that gun. The lever has its small selectable physical shape rather than an invisible full cube. Existing server-authoritative local-block packets validate reach, material revision, current ray, permissions and game mode. Real Survival placement consumes the gun/lever item and uses local hull axes. This is direct mounted-lever control, not a complete redstone-network implementation.

`MountedWeaponEmitter` is shared with the reference batteries and uses the existing WeaponCore/projectile/damage path. It transforms the actual muzzle through the body orientation, inherits material-point velocity (`v + omega cross r`), and applies point recoil to that same physical body. Generic mechanisms use the real firing body as the nonliving projectile owner and damage source. Local armour, world terrain, another hull and unavailable geometry block emission through the muzzle corridor. A lever-controlled reference mount is excluded from the old battery schedule even when switched off, preventing a second controller from firing it.

The existing post-contact world phase owns generic firing. The world budget is 64 mount checks, 32 shots and 32 body visits per frame, with at most 16 checks on one body and round-robin continuation. Laser/rocket cooldowns are 8/24 ticks; missed simulation time does not queue bursts. Timers belong to material cells, survive NBT schema1 loading and committed fracture transfer, and do not reset when a lever is switched off/on. Material edits update the affected mount and six neighbours; initial discovery and changed mount-list rebuilding are separate.

## Contact corrections

Native firing exposed a real coupled-contact failure. A contact time now includes the entire active constraint island, including members that did not directly touch the obstacle. Passive contact rows are interleaved with contacts; the island then consumes its remaining physical time together. Forces and motor work are not integrated twice. Terrain admission and whole-island rollback/pause remain bounded; no entity follows an assigned anchor pose.

Only the two actual mating bearing cells are exempted from their mutual contact pair while their material link exists. Greedy collider primitives are locally refined around those cells and cached against immutable geometry. All other material pairs, unrelated hulls, terrain, actor colliders and picking retain real geometry. Destroying/removing the link restores the pair's ordinary collision behaviour.

Free-motion speed limits now run before constraint projection. Solved pose integration retains the projected velocities and angular momentum rather than clamping each connected body independently afterwards. The ordinary free-body integration path still applies its limits. Corner travel is checked using the actual projected motion, with existing bounded retries/substeps.

A tiny CCD remainder no longer turns an ordinary instantaneous impact into an arbitrarily large inferred breaking load or stabilization frequency. Passive continuation measures strength over the enclosing 50ms frame and bounds its positional-bias time scale. Motors receive no renewed allowance. A separate real-overload unit case still breaks a weak finite joint and checks momentum conservation.
